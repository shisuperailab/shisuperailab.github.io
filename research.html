Methods and applications

Research

The Shi Group is a research group developing physics-informed AI methods, including generative models, language models, AI for science foundation models, and graph learning, to understand, design, and engineer complex polymers, soft materials, macromolecules, biomacromolecular systems, and molecular interfaces. We are particularly interested in developing physics-informed AI models into applications of polymers and biomacromolecules in semiconductor, healthcare, sustainability, energy, and gas separation.

The Shi Group is a fully computational (dry) lab. We do not conduct experiments in-house. When experimental work or validation is needed, it is carried out by experimental collaborators outside our group, connecting our AI methods and computational predictions with experimental evidence.

Representative publications include MacroSimGNN on macromolecular similarity and Measuring Differences in Protein Allosteric Graphs. Both have publicly available code linked on our publications page.

Thrust 1

Developing Conditional Generative Model for Complex Polymer Design

We develop generative models for complex polymers that can reason over chemistry, architecture, topology, and target material properties.

Thrust 2

Developing Environment-aware AI Models to Improve Property and Response Prediction for Complex Polymers

We build predictive AI models that connect polymer structure, physical state, external conditions, and material response across polymer families.

Thrust 3

Developing AI Models to Accelerate and Interpret Complex Polymer Simulation

We integrate molecular simulation, field-based modeling, and machine learning to accelerate sampling, connect scales, and interpret polymer structure-property relationships.

Thrust 4

Developing Physics-informed AI Models to Predict and Design Biomacromolecules with Polymer Physics and Statistical Physics

We combine AI method development with principles from polymer physics and statistical physics to understand and design proteins, RNA, and lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). By incorporating these physical principles into model development, we aim to build AI models that connect molecular structure, interactions, and function to guide prediction and design.

Experimental Collaborators

Polymer Institute, University of Toledo

We collaborate with the Polymer Institute at the University of Toledo to connect AI-driven polymer design and computational predictions with experimental validation. Experimental work is carried out by our collaborators, rather than within the Shi Group. Their measurements and evaluations of designed materials provide feedback to refine our AI methods and guide subsequent designs.
